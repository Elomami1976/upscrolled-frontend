The Short Answer

UpScrolled is a social media app that mixes short videos, photos and text posts, a bit like a cross between Instagram and X (Twitter). Its main selling points are a chronological feed of the accounts you follow, and a stated commitment not to shadowban users or secretly push some posts down. It is free and available on iPhone (App Store) and Android (Google Play).

Who Made UpScrolled?

UpScrolled was founded by Issam Hijazi, a Palestinian-Australian developer born in Jordan who previously worked at large tech companies including IBM and Oracle. He is based in Sydney, and the app is published by his Australian company, Recursive Methods. According to the company, he started building UpScrolled after feeling that meaningful posts were being buried on mainstream platforms while misinformation spread. The project has been supported by the Tech for Palestine incubator.

Why UpScrolled Became Popular

UpScrolled launched quietly in June 2025. Its big moment came in late January 2026, right after TikTok's US business was taken over by a new group of American investors. Many users worried that feeds and moderation would change, especially around political topics, and started looking for alternatives.

UpScrolled's downloads exploded:

  • Downloads jumped by more than 2,800% in a few days around January 22–24, 2026.
  • On January 29, 2026 it became the most downloaded app on Apple's App Store in the US.
  • The company reported about 2.5 million users in early February and about 5 million by April 2026.

Much of the early wave came from people who felt posts about Palestine and other political subjects were being suppressed elsewhere, and the app's trending topics reflected that. Over time it has drawn a wider mix of users.

How the UpScrolled Feed Works

UpScrolled has two main ways to browse:

  • Following: posts from accounts you follow, shown in chronological order (newest first) with no algorithmic ranking.
  • Discover: posts from across the app, organized by topics and surfaced using likes, comments and shares, with newer posts favored and some randomness mixed in.

The company says there is "no manipulation" of reach. This is the biggest difference from TikTok and Instagram, whose main feeds are driven by recommendation algorithms. For a full comparison, see UpScrolled vs TikTok and UpScrolled vs Instagram.

What You Can Post on UpScrolled

  • Text posts with no character limit, plus mentions, hashtags and links
  • Photo posts with up to 10 images
  • Videos from 6 seconds up to 10 minutes long, in vertical (9:16), horizontal (16:9), 4:5 or 4:3 formats
  • Reposts, likes and comments on other people's posts
  • Private messages with other users

Each post can also have a Topic and a location. The built-in editing tools are basic (cropping, reordering photos, previewing), so creators who want effects or captions usually edit elsewhere first.

How to Get Started on UpScrolled

  1. Download UpScrolled from the App Store or Google Play. Check the developer name to avoid look-alike apps.
  2. Create an account and choose a username. Pseudonyms are allowed, but fake or deceptive accounts are not.
  3. Follow a few people or topics so your Following feed has something in it.
  4. Tap the + button at the bottom of the screen and choose Text, Photo or Video to make your first post. Stay in the app until a video finishes uploading.

Ads and Creator Money

UpScrolled is free to use. The company has said it is exploring ads, but that any ads would be run in-house, without third-party ad networks or tracking, and not targeted using personal data.

For creators, UpScrolled has announced plans for creator payouts, branded content and community support tools. As of the latest public information, these were still on the roadmap rather than live, so you can't yet earn money directly from the app the way you can on TikTok, YouTube or Instagram. Be wary of websites claiming otherwise without pointing to an official announcement.

Things to Know Before Joining

UpScrolled's light-touch approach has trade-offs, and it's fair to know them up front:

  • Moderation struggles: the company publicly admitted its moderation "hasn't been able to keep up" with its rapid growth and said it was expanding its Trust & Safety team. News outlets including The Times, the Jewish Telegraphic Agency and Moment reported hateful, extremist and adult content slipping through during 2026.
  • App store hiccup: the app was briefly removed from Google Play on February 14, 2026 and restored the next day.
  • Small team: as a young app, features, stability and support are still catching up with the user base.
  • Political focus: the community leans heavily toward news, politics and activism, which some users love and others find intense.

If you're a parent, note that UpScrolled does not yet have the mature parental controls that larger platforms offer.

Is UpScrolled Worth Trying?

If you're tired of algorithmic feeds and want to see posts from people you follow in order, UpScrolled is worth a look, and it costs nothing to try. Go in knowing it is still a young platform: expect rough edges, uneven moderation, and no creator income yet. Many people use it alongside TikTok or Instagram rather than instead of them.
